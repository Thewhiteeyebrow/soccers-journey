About Soccer's Journey

First edition · November 2026

A map, not a ladder. Every road a player can take through American soccer, drawn in one place, with the numbers where anyone has counted and honest blanks where nobody has.

Most of what families are told about youth soccer is a ladder: rec, then travel, then academy, then college, then pro, and if you fall off you are out. The map says something different. There are five functions a program can serve, four handoffs between them, and dozens of roads that run sideways, backward and out into the adult game, the working game and the rest of a life. Where a road has been measured, the map shows the figure as "of 100" and names the source. Where it hasn't, it says so.

The Five Functions

PLAYRecreation gives you the game. Play, belonging, basic competence. Most of youth soccer lives here and should. TESTCompetition tests you. Commitment, winning, losing, adversity. The game starts asking something back. LEARNDevelopment changes you. Deliberate training, feedback, the right challenge. The objective becomes the player, not the result. CHOOSESelection sorts you. Access to scarce environments, by ability and potential. The earlier the gate, the less it predicts. PATHWAYThe fifth function invests in what survives. A pro contract for a few, a college roster for far more.

Between each pair sits a handoff, and each handoff has one question a family can ask. Does the kid want more? What can the player do in May that they could not in September? Who is grading, and what are they selling? What was measured, and where did the player fall short? The map is built so those questions are never far from wherever your dot is standing.

Where the numbers come from

Every figure on the map carries its source and a confidence letter: A for an observed cohort, B for published official counts, C for a modeled estimate with its assumptions stated. The sources are public: U.S. Soccer, the NCAA, MLS, the SFIA and Aspen Institute participation surveys, and published research on youth sport. Of the roads a population can travel, fewer than one in ten has ever been counted. The rest are drawn as unknown, never as zero, and the season check-in on the map is the instrument that starts counting them.

Your data

The map takes no personal information from or about a child. What it keeps, who can see it, and how you take it or delete it is written in plain words on the privacy page.

Ask the map

The question box answers only from what is on the map: its places, its roads, its sources and the framework text. It cannot invent a number, will not advise a family what to do, and says plainly when nobody has measured something. Ask it where a road leads; do not ask it what to pay for.
