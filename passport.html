Soccer's Journey · The Passport

A record of where you went and what you did, confirmed by the people who were there.

Youth soccer has no memory. Every tryout starts from zero. The passport is the player's own record, kept by the player, confirmed by others, readable by anyone they choose.

1 · The function

Where you played

Clubs, teams, leagues, seasons, camps, tournaments. The places on the map a player actually stood.

Confirmed by the club or organizer.

Where you went

The moves. Tryouts, promotions, cuts, breaks, comebacks, the switch to a new club or to the adult game.

Confirmed by the club on each side of the move.

What you did

Accomplishments. Games, courses, exams, badges, refereeing, coaching younger kids, learning done on FootyEye or in Futbol Island.

Confirmed by a person who was there, or by the product itself.

3 · Four grades, printed on every stamp

The passport never pretends one grade is another. A grade can be added to, never upgraded.

You said it

The player or family pressed it. A diary entry. Honest, unconfirmed.

Product confirmed

FootyEye, Futbol Island or another product recorded it automatically when it happened.

Confirmed by email

A person at the club or organization said yes from the organization's verified address.

Confirmed in person

Someone who was there scanned it on the spot with the organization's account. The strongest grade.

4 · Why it matters in soccer
A tryout is twenty minutes. The coach sees a kid, not a player's history.
→
The passport shows three seasons, two clubs and a comeback, each confirmed by the club that saw it.
Getting cut erases you. The club keeps no record and neither does anyone else.
→
The cut is a stamp. So is the return. The road back is on the record, not just in the family's memory.
Families rely on a highlight reel and their own word.
→
A coach gets a confirmed record instead of a sales pitch. Less to trust, more to check.
Nobody tracks movement between clubs, levels or functions. The map's biggest blank.
→
Every confirmed move is a counted road, with consent, shared back as public totals. The passport is how the map starts counting.
A player leaves the youth game at 13 and the whole record vanishes.
→
The record is theirs. It leaves with them, into the adult game, college, coaching or out of soccer altogether.
5 · Why it matters outside soccer

About 70 of 100 kids have quit by 13. What survives the game is the person. Schools, colleges and employers ask for evidence of exactly the things a soccer journey produces, and nobody has been writing it down.

"Tell us about a time you faced a setback."
→
Cut at 11, confirmed by the club. Back at a different club at 12, confirmed. Three seasons since. Dated, checkable.
"Show us commitment over time."
→
Six seasons of confirmed play across two clubs and a Sunday league. Not a sentence in an essay. A list of stamps.
"Have you led anything? Served anyone?"
→
Captaincy confirmed by the coach. Referee badge confirmed by the association. Forty hours coaching U8s confirmed by the club.
"Can you learn on your own?"
→
FootyEye tactics modules and Futbol Island levels, confirmed by the products at the moment they were earned.
6 · Character: shown, not claimed

Nobody gets to stamp "resilient" on themselves. The passport groups confirmed events under the trait they evidence. The reader draws the conclusion. That is the only version a recruiter will trust.

Resilience

  • Cut, then returned
  • Injury, then a full season back
  • Stayed after a relegation

Commitment

  • Seasons in a row at one club
  • Games played per season
  • Years in the game, any level

Leadership and service

  • Captaincy
  • Coaching or refereeing younger players
  • Volunteer hours at the club

Learning

  • Courses and exams passed
  • Product-confirmed modules
  • Moves into a new function (Play to Learn, Test to Choose)

Range

  • Positions played
  • Formats: 11v11, futsal, Sunday league
  • Roles beyond player: ref, coach, organizer

Judgment

  • Chose to step down a level and stayed in the game
  • Took a break and came back
  • Left the pathway for the adult game on purpose
7 · One record, three readers

The holder builds a view for a purpose and shares a link that works for 30 days. Each reader sees the stamps chosen for them and can check every one without an account.

For a club

Where you played, the moves, the games. The soccer record.

The reader can scan any stamp and see who confirmed it.

For a school or college

Seasons, setbacks and comebacks, service, learning. The character record.

Same stamps, grouped by trait, no scouting language.

For an employer

Commitment over time, roles held, courses passed, people led.

Works even if the reader has never watched a soccer game.

8 · Rules that don't bend
The player owns itHeld on their device. They choose every stamp that gets shared. They can delete the whole passport.
No child gives an emailConfirmers are organizations. Their address is the organization's. The note that tells a club who the player is lives in the email only and is never kept.
Grades never upgradeEmail stays email. "You said it" stays "you said it". Adding a confirmation is the only way up.
Nothing is quietly erasedA club can say "not us". The stamp stays, marked disputed, with both sides visible.
Checkable without usEvery stamp carries its own proof and the day it was recorded. If Soccer's Journey vanished, the stamps still check.
Confirmers see one lineA club confirming a tryout sees the tryout. Not the rest of the passport, not the other clubs, not the cuts.
9 · What it is not
Not a rating. No scores, no stars, no rank. A record of what happened, not an opinion of how good someone is.
Not a scouting report. No coach writes an evaluation in it. A confirmation is a yes to a fact, nothing more.
Not a highlight reel. No video, no stats that flatter. Games played, not goals scored.
Not a reference letter. It replaces "trust me" with "check it". The reader still does the judging.
